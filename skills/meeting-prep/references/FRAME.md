# Frame — what this skill is, and the rules it never breaks

## The problem
A day of back-to-back meetings. Work accumulates faster than there's time
to do it. By the time a gap opens, the context is gone.

## What this is NOT
- **Not a notetaker.** Notetakers already summarize meetings well.
- **Not a meeting summarizer.** A summary is an *input* here, never the
  output.

## What this IS
A skill that maintains **topics** and prepares you for what's next. The
question it answers is *"what do I need for my 2pm"* — not *"what happened
in my 10am"*.

## Core model: topics, not meetings
Obligations don't live inside one meeting. They live across a topic: the
same pricing question comes up in Monday's sync, Wednesday's customer call,
and a Slack thread in between.

- The unit is a **topic**, not a meeting.
- Meetings, threads, emails, tickets and docs are **events** filed into a
  topic.
- A topic accumulates state: decisions made, open questions, who owes what.

## Five rules that hold it together

**1. Never auto-create a topic.**
The person knows what their topics are. Unmatched material goes to the
inbox for them to file. Auto-creation is where confident nonsense starts.

**2. Never auto-merge two topics.**
Adjacent is not the same. "Enterprise pricing" and "the Halvorsen renewal"
touch the same customer and are different topics with different owners.

**3. A topic file is a maintained digest, never an append-only log.**
Decisions, open questions, commitments. Source material is *referenced*,
not pasted in — or the file outgrows what's useful to load.

**4. Draft everything, send nothing.**
Messages, emails and invites are drafted for the person to send. Nothing
is ever sent by the skill.

**5. Every correction is remembered.**
When the person corrects something, one line of *what* and *why* goes in
the working folder's `lessons.md`, and is read on every later run.

## The success bar

> **Did this beat a blank page plus twenty minutes?**

A 70%-right brief costs as much to fix as one written from scratch.

## Verification is not optional
Three small scripts and one independent reader check every brief before the person sees it:

- **`factcheck.js`** — every quoted line is verbatim in the source it
  cites; every paragraph number exists; every cross-reference lands; every
  figure traces to a source or is flagged. A FAIL means the brief is
  wrong, not debatable: a fabricated quotation with a source attached is
  worse than an omission, because it walks you into a room confident you
  can back up words nobody said.
- **`score.js`** — the shape: length, the four sections, a decision you
  could say out loud, a real time axis, callouts that lead with the bottom
  line, steps that stand alone.
- **`coverage.js`** — every key finding in a topic file is used in the
  brief or set aside out loud. A finding filed and silently dropped is the
  most common way a brief loses its best evidence.
- **The independent reader** (`READER-PROMPT.md`) — a separate model reads
  the raw sources, never the topic files, and lists what must be in the
  brief before it reads it. It catches what the writer's own frame hid.

The pattern these exist for: the system is reliable on facts it retrieved
and unreliable on everything it writes between them.
